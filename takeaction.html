Take Action

Discover simple and meaningful ways to help protect our natural environments. Through responsible daily habits and community involvement, everyone can contribute to keeping mangroves, rivers, lakes, and oceans clean and healthy.

Ways You Can Help

Sustainable Daily Practices

Reducing Plastic Use

Individuals can help reduce plastic pollution by making more responsible choices in their everyday activities. Bringing reusable shopping bags, water bottles, and food containers can reduce the need for disposable plastic products. People can also avoid unnecessary plastic packaging and choose reusable alternatives whenever possible. These small changes can be practiced at home, in school, and when shopping. By using fewer disposable products and reusing items whenever appropriate, individuals can help reduce the amount of plastic waste that may eventually reach rivers, lakes, and oceans.

Proper Waste Management

Proper waste management begins with responsible handling of the waste produced at home, in school, and in the community. Individuals can help by separating biodegradable, recyclable, residual, and special waste according to local waste-management guidelines. Recyclable materials should be placed in appropriate collection areas, while biodegradable waste may be composted when facilities are available. Waste should never be thrown into streets, drainage systems, rivers, or lakes. Practicing proper waste segregation, recycling, and disposal helps reduce the amount of waste that can reach aquatic environments and contributes to cleaner surroundings.

Individual Environmental Actions

Keeping Waterways Clean

Keeping rivers, lakes, and coastal areas clean can begin with simple actions in our surroundings. Individuals should avoid throwing garbage into streets, drainage systems, and bodies of water, as improperly disposed waste can be carried into nearby waterways. When ordinary litter is found along accessible riverbanks, lakeshores, or coastal areas, people can help by collecting it and placing it in the proper waste containers, provided it is safe to do so. Supporting proper waste disposal and keeping nearby surroundings clean can help prevent waste from entering aquatic environments and reduce pollution that affects wildlife and natural habitats.

  • National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, Fisheries. (n.d.). What you can do for habitat.

Community Participation

Joining School and University Environmental Clubs

Students can participate in environmental protection by joining school or university organizations that promote environmental awareness and conservation. Some student organizations take part in activities such as coastal clean-ups, tree planting, and environmental campaigns. Through these organizations, students can work with their classmates and other volunteers in supporting environmental initiatives within their schools and communities. Those interested can ask their school administration or student affairs office about available environmental organizations and upcoming activities.

Participating in NSTP and Community Clean-up Activities

Students can also contribute to environmental protection through activities organized under the National Service Training Program (NSTP) and other school or local initiatives. Depending on the programs offered by their institution, students may have opportunities to participate in coastal clean-ups, mangrove planting, waste collection, and environmental awareness activities. They can also join clean-up drives organized by local government units and environmental groups. Through these activities, students can work alongside community members to maintain cleaner surroundings and support the protection of nearby rivers, lakes, and coastal environments.
